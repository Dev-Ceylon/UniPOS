1Introducing UniPOS

UniPOS is a modular, offline-first point of sale for small businesses — retail shops, groceries, restaurants, cafés and service businesses. It runs in any modern browser on a PC, tablet or phone, keeps selling when the internet drops, and gives owners live numbers from anywhere.

It was designed for Sri Lankan businesses first — Sri Lankan rupees, VAT that is already included in shelf prices, and English, Sinhala (සිංහල) and Tamil (தமிழ்) — and it works the same way in any country and currency.

Who it is for

PersonWhat UniPOS gives them
CashierA fast, scanner-first register with big buttons, keyboard shortcuts and a 4–6 digit PIN to start a shift. It keeps working offline.
Owner / managerToday's sales and profit, low-stock alerts, staff and role control, refunds and voids under permission, and reports exportable to CSV and PDF.
Inventory clerkStock levels per location, adjustments with reasons, stocktakes with a scanner, transfers between branches and purchase orders.
Kitchen staffA kitchen display that shows orders by table and course, with one big button to start, finish and bump each ticket.

Highlights

Sell in seconds

Scan, tap, pay. A three-item cash sale takes under 15 seconds.

Never lose a sale

Sales are saved on the device first and sync automatically — exactly once.

Exact money

Totals, VAT and refunds are calculated to the cent, the same way on every device.

One app, many businesses

Switch between retail and restaurant modes, or turn single features on and off.

Safe by design

Each business only ever sees its own data. Sensitive actions need permission and are logged.

Yours to run

Open source (MIT). Self-host with Docker or deploy to a cloud host.

3How UniPOS works

UniPOS is one web application. Every device — a counter PC, a tablet, the kitchen screen or the owner's phone — opens it in a browser. The register keeps a copy of everything it needs on the device so it can sell without the internet.

The life of a sale

  1. The cashier scans items. Prices and taxes come from the catalogue cached on the device, so search is instant (under 100 ms for 10,000 products).
  2. On payment, the register gives the sale its final ID and receipt number, saves it on the device, and prints or shows the receipt immediately — no waiting for the network.
  3. The sale goes into an outbox. When online, it is sent to the server within seconds; when offline, it waits and retries automatically.
  4. The server checks the cashier and permissions, recalculates the totals and tax to confirm them, records the payments, and takes the items out of stock in the stock ledger.
  5. If the same sale arrives twice (for example after a dropped connection), the server recognises it and ignores the copy. Unusual sales — selling below zero stock, unauthorised price changes or big discounts — are flagged for review rather than lost.

Principles that keep the numbers right

PrincipleWhat it means for you
Money in whole centsAmounts are never rounded wrongly; split discounts and refunds always add up exactly.
One tax engine everywhereVAT-inclusive and exclusive prices, compound and regional taxes give the same result on every device and in every report.
Append-only stock ledgerEvery stock change is a dated entry with a reason — sale, return, adjustment, receipt, transfer, stocktake. On-hand is always the sum of the history.
Separated businessesEvery record belongs to one business, and the database itself blocks one business from reading another's data.
Permissions and audit logRefunds, voids, price overrides, large discounts, settings and staff changes need the right permission and are written to the audit log.

4Getting started

Setting up a new business takes a few minutes. You need the address of your UniPOS installation (for example https://pos.yourshop.lk) — see Installation and hardware if you are installing it yourself.

Step 1 — Create your account

  1. Open UniPOS in your browser and choose Create account.
  2. Enter your name, email and a password (at least 8 characters), then press Create account.

Step 2 — Set up your business

  1. Enter the business name and choose the business type (Retail shop, Grocery, Restaurant, Café or Services). This switches on the right features.
  2. Choose the currency, time zone and language, and name your first location.
  3. Enter your sales tax — for Sri Lankan VAT: name VAT, rate 18, and tick Prices include tax.
  4. Choose your 4–6 digit PIN. You will use it to start selling at the register.
  5. Tick Add demo products if you want sample products to try things out, then press Create business.

Step 3 — Your dashboard

UniPOS opens the dashboard. The left menu shows only the features enabled for your business type and allowed for your role. Use the top bar to switch location, switch cashier, change theme or language, see sync status, and sign out.

Signing in later

Sign in with your email and password. A shop device can stay signed in all day; cashiers then switch in with their PIN (see Selling at the register).

Tip. Install UniPOS as an app: in Chrome or Edge choose Install UniPOS from the address bar (on Android: Add to Home screen). It then opens full-screen and starts even without internet.

5Selling at the register

The register is designed for speed: the search box is always ready for a scanner, and everything can be done with a mouse, touch or the keyboard.

Step 1 — Add products

  1. Scan a barcode with a USB scanner — it types into the search box and adds the item automatically.
  2. Or type part of a name or SKU and tap the product. Press Enter to add when there is only one match.
  3. Or tap a favourite or pick a category tab. The camera button scans barcodes with the device camera (Chrome/Edge).

Step 2 — Adjust the sale

  1. Use − / + or type a quantity. Tap a line to select it; the selected line shows extra buttons.
  2. % gives a line discount, the tag changes the price (managers), and the bin removes the line.
  3. Discount under the totals applies a discount to the whole order. Discounts above your limit (20% by default) need a manager.

Step 3 — Add a customer (optional)

Tap Walk-in under Current sale to search customers by name, phone or email, or create a new one. Customers earn loyalty points and appear on the receipt.

Step 4 — Take payment

  1. Press Pay (or F4). The amount due is filled in.
  2. Choose a tender. For cash, tap a quick-cash button (exact amount or the next note). For card, take the payment on your card terminal and type the last four digits as the reference.
  3. To split a payment, add several tenders — for example LKR 1,000 by card and the rest in cash. Change is given only from cash.
  4. Gift cards and loyalty points can also be used as tenders.
  5. Press Complete sale.

Step 5 — Receipt and next sale

The change due is shown in large text with the receipt. Choose Print receipt (80 mm or 58 mm), Email receipt, or press Enter for the next sale.

Hold and resume

Press F8 (or the pause button) to park a sale while a customer fetches something, then serve the next customer. Press F9 (or the play button) to bring it back.

Switching cashier with a PIN

Tap the name in the top bar and enter your PIN. Sales are then recorded under your name until someone else switches in. After five wrong PINs, that PIN is locked for 15 minutes and the attempt is logged.

When the internet is down

A yellow bar says Offline. Keep selling: sales are saved on the device and the counter in the top bar shows how many are waiting. They sync automatically when the connection returns — tap the counter to sync immediately. Creating customers, checking gift-card balances and switching cashier need a connection.

6Sales history, receipts and returns

Finding a sale

Open Sales. Filter by date range, show only refunds, voided or flagged sales, or search by receipt number or customer. Sales that are still waiting to sync on this device are shown at the top.

Sale details

The sale page shows items, discounts, taxes, payments, the customer, any refunds, and review flags. From here you can print, download a PDF, email, refund or void.

Refunding items

  1. On the sale page press Refund.
  2. Enter the quantity to refund for each item. UniPOS shows how many are still refundable and never allows refunding more than was sold.
  3. Choose where the money goes (original payment methods, cash, card…) and enter a reason.
  4. Keep Return items to stock ticked if they can be sold again, then press Refund.

The refund is a separate receipt (numbered RF-…) linked to the original sale, so reports stay correct without special cases. Void cancels a whole sale rung up by mistake and reverses stock; it needs the Void permission and a reason.

Reprinting a receipt

Press Print receipt on any sale for the 80 mm layout, or PDF to download it.

7Shifts and cash-up

  1. At the start of the day open Shifts (or press Open shift on the register) and enter the opening float.
  2. During the day record cash in and cash out (for example, buying milk for the shop) with a reason.
  3. At closing, count the drawer by denomination. UniPOS shows the counted total and the variance against the expected cash as you type.
  4. Press Close shift. The result is saved in the shift history and the audit log.

Expected cash = opening float + cash sales − cash refunds + cash in − cash out. The Takings by tender list shows card, wallet and gift-card totals to reconcile against your terminal.

8Products, taxes and labels

The product list

Open Products to search by name, SKU or barcode and filter by category. Each variant shows its price and stock at the current location.

Adding a product

  1. Press New product and enter the name, category, tax class and type (standard, kit or service).
  2. Fill in the variant row: SKU, barcode, price and cost. Press the wand to generate an in-store barcode if the product has none.
  3. Add more variants (sizes, colours) with + Variant. Enter opening stock if you track stock.
  4. Tick Show in register favourites for fast sellers, then press Save product.

Taxes

Products → Taxes manages tax classes. Each class has one or more rules with a rate, Inclusive (already in the price) or exclusive (added at checkout), Compound (tax on tax), an order and an optional region matched against the location's tax region.

Importing and exporting

Import accepts a CSV file (download the template on the page). Rows are matched by SKU and updated, or created; rows with the same name become variants of one product; stock is set through the stock ledger. Export CSV downloads the whole catalogue for spreadsheets.

Printing barcode labels

Labels prints labels with name, price and barcode on A4 label sheets (3 × 8) or a label printer. Filter by name and choose the number of copies.

9Inventory, stocktakes and transfers

Stock levels

Inventory shows on-hand quantities at the current location, reorder points, value at cost and badges for low or negative stock. Totals at the top show the number of items, units, stock value and low-stock count.

Adjusting stock

  1. Press Adjust on a row.
  2. Choose Add or remove (e.g. −2) or Set counted quantity.
  3. Pick a reason — correction, damaged/expired, received without PO, customer return — add a note and save.

Every adjustment becomes a dated entry in the product's stock ledger (shown at the bottom of each product page) and in the audit log. Edit a reorder point directly in the table to get low-stock alerts on the dashboard.

Running a stocktake

  1. Open Inventory → Stocktakes and press Start stocktake; choose all products or one category.
  2. Count items: type the counted quantity, or scan each item to add +1.
  3. Press Save progress to continue later, then Complete. Only the differences are posted to stock, so sales made during the count are not lost.

Moving stock between locations

Add locations in Settings → Locations (each has its own stock, receipt header and tax region).

  1. Open Transfers and press New transfer. Choose the destination and scan or search the items to send.
  2. Press Create draft, then Send. Stock leaves the source and is in transit.
  3. At the destination press Receive and confirm the quantities. Anything not received returns to the source.

10Suppliers and purchase orders

  1. Open Purchasing and add your suppliers (name, contact, phone, email, address).
  2. Press New purchase order, choose the supplier and expected date, and add items with quantity and unit cost.
  3. Press Create draft, then Mark ordered when you send it.
  4. When goods arrive press Receive stock and enter what arrived. Partial deliveries are allowed; the order shows partial until everything is received. Receiving updates each product's cost.

11Customers, loyalty and gift cards

Customers

Customers lists everyone with visits, lifetime spend and points. Create customers here or at the register. Each profile shows purchase history, notes, custom fields, points history and gift cards.

Loyalty points

Turn loyalty on in Settings → Business → Loyalty: set how much a customer spends per point, what one point is worth and the minimum to redeem. Points are earned automatically when a customer is on the sale, reversed on refunds, and can be used as a tender. Managers can adjust points with a reason.

Gift cards

Gift cards → Issue gift card creates a card with a value, an optional expiry and a scannable code (printable). At the register choose the Gift card tender and scan or type the code; the balance is checked and reduced automatically.

Personal data

On a customer's page, Export data downloads everything held about them and Erase anonymises their profile while keeping sales for your accounts. Record marketing consent on each profile and set a privacy notice and retention period in Settings.

12Dashboard and reports

Dashboard

The dashboard shows today's sales, transactions, average sale and gross profit compared with yesterday, sales by hour, top products, recent sales, low stock and sales that need review.

Reports

  1. Open Reports and choose a date range and location (or all locations).
  2. Pick a report: Sales (by day), Products & profit, Categories, Staff, Tax, Tenders or Inventory valuation.
  3. Press Export CSV for spreadsheets or Export PDF for a printable report for your accountant.

13Quotations and expenses

Quotations

  1. Open Quotations → New quotation, choose a customer, add products and adjust prices.
  2. Set Valid until and a note, then press Create. Use Print / PDF to send it.
  3. When the customer accepts, press Convert to sale: the quote opens in the register with its prices, ready to pay.

Expenses

Expenses → Add expense records rent, utilities, salaries and other costs with a category, amount, date and reference. The page totals the month and breaks it down by category.

14Restaurant and café mode

Choosing the Restaurant or Café preset switches the register to a tables-first layout, adds modifiers and courses, and turns on the kitchen display.

Step 1 — Open a table

Tap a table at the top of the register (or Takeaway). For a new table, choose the number of guests. Tables with an open bill show an orange dot.

Step 2 — Add dishes with options

Dishes with modifier groups open an options dialog: choose the spice level and extras, add a kitchen note (“No onions”) and the course.

Step 3 — Send to the kitchen

Press Send to kitchen. New items go to the kitchen display, grouped by course; sent items are marked Sent and cannot be reduced below what was sent. The table's bill is saved on the server, so any device can continue it.

Step 4 — The kitchen display

Open Kitchen display on a screen in the kitchen. Each ticket shows the table, course, items, options and notes. Press Start, then Ready, then Bump. Tickets older than 15 minutes turn red. Recall brings back the last bumped ticket.

Step 5 — Pay

Press Pay on the table's bill as for any sale. The table becomes free and the receipt shows the table number.

Setting up tables and modifiers

Tables & menu is a floor-plan editor: add tables, set seats, area and shape, and drag them to match your room. Products → Modifiers manages modifier groups (for example Spice level: choose one; Extras: up to three, each with an extra price); attach groups to products on the product page.

15Settings and administration

Business settings and presets

Settings → Business holds the business name, currency, language, time zone, the discount limit without a manager, stock rules, loyalty settings and the privacy notice. Business type applies a preset — retail, grocery, restaurant, café or services — which switches modules, register layout and receipt defaults without losing data.

Staff and roles

  1. In Settings → Staff & roles, type an email, choose a role and press Send invite. The person receives a link, creates an account and chooses their PIN.
  2. Press Edit on a staff member to change their role, reset their PIN (which also unlocks it), limit them to certain locations or disable them.
  3. Use the built-in roles — Owner, Manager, Cashier, Inventory clerk — or press New role and tick exactly the permissions you want.

Modules

Settings → Modules turns features on or off. Modules that depend on others switch together (Loyalty needs Customers; Transfers and Purchasing need Inventory). The menu, register and receipts adapt immediately.

Receipt template

Edit the title, header and footer lines, paper width (80 or 58 mm), and whether to show the tax breakdown, cashier, customer, table and barcode — with a live preview. The location's address and receipt header (for example your VAT number) are added automatically.

Custom fields and theme

Custom fields adds your own text, number, yes/no, date or choice fields to products and customers. Theme sets your brand colour (with a contrast check so text stays readable) and corner style. Each person can switch light/dark mode and language from the top bar.

Backup, restore and audit log

Backup downloads every record for your business as one file; Restore replaces the business data with a backup (type RESTORE to confirm). The Audit log lists refunds, voids, price changes, stock adjustments, settings and staff changes, PIN lock-outs and backups — who did what, and when.

16Security and data protection

AreaHow UniPOS protects it
Separation between businessesEvery record carries its business ID, and the database enforces row-level security, so one business can never read or change another's data — tested automatically, including with deliberately forged queries.
Sign-inPasswords are stored as secure hashes; repeated sign-in attempts are rate-limited. Optional Google sign-in.
Cashier PINsHashed, rate-limited, locked for 15 minutes after five wrong tries, and every switch is logged.
PermissionsEvery action on the server checks a permission before touching data; the screen hides what a role cannot do.
Sensitive actionsRefunds, voids, price overrides, large discounts, stock adjustments, settings, staff changes and backups are written to the audit log.
Sales integrityThe server recalculates every synced sale and rejects tampered totals; unusual sales are flagged for review.
Personal dataConsent flag, privacy notice, retention setting, per-customer export and erase (for Sri Lanka's Personal Data Protection Act — confirm obligations with your legal adviser).
Web securityStrict content security policy, no framing, safe CSV export, automated dependency and code scanning.

17Installation and hardware

Running UniPOS

UniPOS needs a server (Node.js) and a PostgreSQL database. The easiest options:

OptionBest forHow
Docker ComposeSelf-hosting on your own PC or serverCopy .env.example to .env, set a secret, run docker compose up -d, open port 3000.
Vercel + Neon or SupabaseManaged cloud, no servers to look afterCreate the database, run the migrations, import the repository in Vercel and set the environment variables.
Railway or RenderSimple managed container hostingDeploy the Dockerfile with a Postgres add-on and run migrations before each release.
Note. Full steps are in docs/deployment.md in the source code. GitHub Pages cannot run UniPOS because it only serves static files.
